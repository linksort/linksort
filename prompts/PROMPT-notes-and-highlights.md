## 1. How notes work today (so you know what you are replacing)

Backend:
- `model.Link` has `Annotation string` and `IsAnnotated bool`. `db.LinkStore.UpdateLink` sets `IsAnnotated = len(Annotation) > 0` on every save (`ReplaceOne` of the whole struct). There is an index on `isannotated`.
- Notes are written through the general `PATCH /api/links/{id}` (`handler/link.UpdateLinkRequest.Annotation *string`, applied by reflection in `controller/link.UpdateLink`). `GET /api/links?annotated=1` filters to annotated links (`db.GetLinksAnnotated`). The assistant's `get_links` tool exposes the same filter (`assistant/assistant.go`).
- The text search index (`db/client.go SetupIndexes`) covers `corpus`, `title`, `description`, `site`; **notes are not indexed**, even though the splash site says "Notes are searchable".
- The public API docs (`docs/`, generated by `swag`) and the iOS app consume `annotation` / `isAnnotated`; keep those fields and their semantics backward compatible.

Frontend (`frontend/src/pages/LinkView.jsx`):
- The note editor is a **fixed bar at the bottom of the viewport**. Hidden state: one labeled button, "Add a note" / "Show note". Open state: a bare auto-growing `Textarea` (`frontend/src/components/Textarea.jsx`) above a row with Hide (↓) and Expand/Shrink icon buttons, the word "Note", and "Saving…/Saved" on the right. Three panel states (`hidden`, `normal` at 13rem max height, `maximized` at calc(100vh - 8rem)).
- Saving is automatic: `useDebounce(annotation, 1000)` → `useLinkOperations(link).handleSaveAnnotation` → `useUpdateLink` (PATCH with `annotation`). A `hasLoadedAnnotationRef` timestamp guards against saving the initial load. `useUpdateLink` updates the `["links","detail",id]` and current `["links","list",…]` caches.
- The sidebar has a "Notes" filter (`?annotated=1`, `useFilters().makeToggleAnnotationsLink`, `areAnnotationsShowing`); `NullScreen` has an empty state for it ("You haven't added any notes yet."). **Nothing on the link cards (tile/comfy/condensed) indicates that a link has a note**, and the Notes view shows the same cards as the All view, with no note text.
- The article is rendered with `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` from `link.corpus` (HTML from the extractor, wrapped in `.prose`, 65ch max width, Georgia). Links without a long corpus fall back to the `description` paragraph or an "Open the original" line.

Known critique of the current notes UI (`.impeccable/critique/*`): the editor is hidden behind unlabeled icons at the bottom; the note is a second-class, invisible object in the list views; nothing surfaces it anywhere except the link page.

## 2. Product intent

- Notes answer "why did I save this?" and highlights answer "what mattered in it?". Both should be **visible where the link is** (the link page, the cards, the Notes view, search) and **never require filing or deciding anything up front** (PRODUCT.md principles 1–3).
- Highlights are **light yellow**, like a highlighter on paper, which fits DESIGN.md's index-card metaphor. This is a deliberate exception to "The One Pen Rule" (blue is for actions; yellow is content the user made). Define one token for it in `theme.js` (e.g. `highlight: "#FFF3B0"`-ish; verify body text on it is ≥ 4.5:1 against `gray.800`) and use it everywhere a highlight appears: the article, cards, the Notes view, and the splash site later. A hovered/selected highlight may deepen one step; nothing else is yellow.
- A highlight **without** a note is a complete, valid object (most highlights will have no note). A highlight **with** a note shows the note next to the highlighted text.
- The link-level note stays: it is the note "about the link", separate from highlight notes.
- No new typeface, no shadows except for things that float (the selection toolbar and popovers), 0.5rem radius on controls, 0.75rem on cards, hover by tint.

## 3. Three UI options: present them, then build one

Before writing code, write up the three options below in your own words with a quick ASCII or screenshot-mockup for each (you may render static mockups in the running app with throwaway JSX to take screenshots), list trade-offs, state your recommendation, and **ask the user to choose**. If you are running unattended and cannot get an answer, build **Option B** and say so in your summary. Whichever option is chosen, the data model, API, anchoring utilities, list-view treatment, and search changes in §4–§7 are the same.

### Option A: "Margin notes" (reading-first, notes in the gutter)

On wide screens the article column (`maxWidth="50rem"` inside a `5xl` container, so there is ~14rem of unused width from `xl` up) grows a right-hand **annotation rail**. Each highlight's note is a small white card in the rail, vertically aligned with its highlight (like margin comments in Medium/Google Docs; cards push each other down when they would overlap). The link note is the first card in the rail, titled "Your note", always present (empty state: "Add a note about this link…"). Selecting text in the article shows a floating pill with two actions: **Highlight** and **Highlight + note**. Clicking a highlight focuses its card. Below `xl` the rail collapses: notes render inline beneath the paragraph that contains their highlight, and the link note sits under the Summary card.

- Pros: the most "paper" of the three; notes live beside the text they refer to; nothing is hidden.
- Cons: the alignment logic (measure highlight `getBoundingClientRect`, lay out cards, resolve overlaps on resize/font load) is the most work and the most fragile; two layouts to maintain (rail vs inline).

### Option B: "Notebook panel" (recommended default)

Replace the fixed bottom bar with a **Notebook panel** that belongs to the link page: a toggleable column on the right edge of the content area (inside the content column, not the global chat panel; width ~20rem from `lg` up), and a bottom drawer (Chakra `Drawer placement="bottom"`) below `lg`. The toolbar's "Add a note" button becomes a **"Notebook"** toggle with a count badge ("Notebook · 3"). The panel contains, top to bottom:

1. **Your note**: an auto-growing textarea with the existing debounced auto-save and a quiet "Saved" / "Saving…" status. Always present; placeholder "Why did you save this?".
2. **Highlights**: one card per highlight in document order: a yellow left edge, the quoted text (clamped to 3 lines, expandable), the optional note beneath in Ink Soft, and a `⋯` menu (Add/Edit note, Copy text, Remove highlight). Clicking a card scrolls the article to the highlight and flashes it (deepen the yellow for 600ms, reduced-motion safe). Empty state: "Select some text in the article to highlight it."

Selecting text in the article shows a floating pill with **Highlight** and **Highlight + note** (the latter creates the highlight and opens its note field in the panel with focus). Clicking a highlight in the article opens a small popover anchored to it showing its note (if any) with Edit/Remove; "Edit" focuses the card in the panel.

- Pros: simplest robust implementation; one component works on every width; the panel is a natural index of everything the user wrote about this link; easy to reuse the card for the Notes list view.
- Cons: notes are not spatially beside the text (mitigated by click-to-scroll and the popover on the highlight).

### Option C: "Inline annotations" (everything in the reading flow)

No panel and no bar. The link note is an always-visible **"Your note" block** directly under the Summary card (click to edit, auto-save; collapsed to one line when not focused). Highlight notes render as **inline callouts** directly after the paragraph containing the highlight: a yellow-edged line in Body Small with the note text, so the page reads like an annotated printout. Selecting text shows the same floating pill. A thin "Highlights (3)" summary line in the title block jumps to the first highlight.

- Pros: nothing to open; the page is the notebook; least chrome.
- Cons: callouts interrupt the reading flow and need care on paragraphs with several highlights; no single list of highlights per link on the page (the Notes view provides it); editing a callout in place is fiddly on phones.

## 4. Data model and API (backend)

Add highlights to the link document. Anchoring uses **text quotes**, the way the W3C Web Annotation model does (`TextQuoteSelector`), so highlights survive re-rendering and small changes in markup:

```go
// model/link.go
type Highlight struct {
    ID        string    `json:"id"`                 // uuid, server-generated
    Exact     string    `json:"exact"`              // the highlighted text (textContent, whitespace-normalized); max 2000 chars
    Prefix    string    `json:"prefix"`             // up to 64 chars of text before, for disambiguation
    Suffix    string    `json:"suffix"`             // up to 64 chars after
    Start     int       `json:"start"`              // character offset of Exact in the article's text content (a hint; may drift)
    End       int       `json:"end"`
    Note      string    `json:"note"`               // optional; max 10,000 chars
    CreatedAt time.Time `json:"createdAt"`
    UpdatedAt time.Time `json:"updatedAt"`
}

// on model.Link
Highlights []Highlight `json:"highlights"`
```

- `IsAnnotated` becomes `len(Annotation) > 0 || len(Highlights) > 0` (set in `db.LinkStore.UpdateLink` as today). Keep the `isannotated` index. `annotated=1` therefore returns links with a note *or* highlights.
- Cap highlights at 200 per link (400 Bad Request beyond). Reject `exact` that is empty/whitespace.
- The `GetLinksByUser` projection (`db/link.go`) must include `annotation` and `highlights` so list views can render note excerpts and highlight counts without an extra request. If you are worried about payload size, project `highlights.exact`, `highlights.note`, `highlights.id` only; do not project `corpus`.
- New endpoints in `handler/link/link.go` (document each with the same `godoc` swagger comments the file already uses):
  - `POST /api/links/{linkID}/highlights` with `{exact, prefix, suffix, start, end, note}` → `201 {"link": …}` (the whole updated link, consistent with the other link endpoints).
  - `PATCH /api/links/{linkID}/highlights/{highlightID}` with `{note}` → `200 {"link": …}`.
  - `DELETE /api/links/{linkID}/highlights/{highlightID}` → `200 {"link": …}`.
  - All go through a controller method that loads the link, checks `link.UserID == u.ID` (404 otherwise, as `GetLink` does), mutates, and `UpdateLink`s. Wrap in `Transactor.DoInTransaction` to avoid lost updates when two highlights are created quickly in a row (read-modify-write on the whole document).
- Keep `PATCH /api/links/{id}` with `annotation` working exactly as before (iOS and public API).
- **Search**: notes and highlights must be searchable. Add `annotation`, `highlights.exact`, and `highlights.note` to the text index in `db/client.go`. MongoDB allows **one** text index per collection, and `CreateMany` with a different key spec on the existing index name fails, so `SetupIndexes` must detect the existing text index (list indexes, find the one with `textIndexVersion`), compare its key/weights spec, and drop + recreate it when it differs. This runs at startup in prod; make it idempotent and log what it did. Give `title` and `annotation`/`highlights.note` higher weights than `corpus` (e.g. title 10, annotation 6, highlights 6, description 3, site 3, corpus 1); a separate task is researching search quality in depth, so keep this change minimal and well-commented.
- **Assistant**: `assistant/assistant.go`'s `get_link` tool output should include the note and the highlights (exact + note) so the chat can answer "what did I highlight in that article?"; update the feature list in the system prompt ("Annotations" → "Notes and highlights"). Do not change the model id.
- Regenerate Swagger: `swag init --dir cmd/serve/,handler,model,payload,agent` (swag `v1.8.10`, per AGENTS.md).

## 5. Anchoring and rendering highlights (frontend)

Put the pure logic in `frontend/src/utils/highlights.js` and unit-test it with jest/jsdom (`CI=true yarn test`):

- `textIndex(root)`: walk the text nodes under the article root with a `TreeWalker`, returning the concatenated text and a map from global offsets to `{node, offset}`. Normalize whitespace consistently in both directions (collapse runs of whitespace to one space) so `exact/prefix/suffix` round-trip.
- `describeSelection(root, selection)` → `{exact, prefix, suffix, start, end}` or `null` when the selection is empty, collapsed, or not inside `root`.
- `locate(root, highlight)` → a `Range` or `null`: find `exact`; if it occurs more than once, pick the occurrence whose prefix/suffix match best; use `start` as a tiebreaker; return `null` when the text is not found (the highlight is "orphaned"; still list it in the panel with an "Text no longer found" hint; never crash).
- `wrapRange(range, id)`: wrap the range in `<mark data-highlight-id=… class="ls-highlight">` elements, **one per text node segment** (a range can span several nodes and `surroundContents` throws on partial containment). Provide `unwrapAll(root)` so highlights can be re-applied after the article re-renders.

Then in the link page:

- A `useHighlights(link)` hook (`frontend/src/hooks/highlights.js`) exposes `highlights`, `createHighlight`, `updateHighlightNote`, `removeHighlight`, each a react-query mutation that updates the `["links","detail",id]` cache from the returned link (follow `useUpdateLink`'s pattern) and invalidates `["links","list"]` without refetching active queries (`refetchActive: false`, as `useCreateLink` does).
- An `ArticleHighlights`-style effect applies `locate + wrapRange` for every highlight after the article HTML mounts and whenever `link.highlights` changes; it must be idempotent (`unwrapAll` first) and must not re-run while the user is mid-selection.
- Styling: `mark.ls-highlight { background: <token>; color: inherit; border-radius: 2px; padding: 0 1px; cursor: pointer; }`, hover/focused state one step deeper. Marks are keyboard reachable (`tabindex="0"`, `role="button"`, `aria-label="Highlight: {exact, truncated}{; note: …}"`), Enter/Space opens the popover. Add the rule in `prose.css` next to the other `.prose` rules.
- **Selection toolbar**: listen to `selectionchange` (debounced ~150ms) and `mouseup`/`touchend` on the article root; when `describeSelection` returns a result, show a floating pill (white, Pencil border, the 24px floating glow from DESIGN.md, `3xl` radius) positioned above the selection's bounding rect (below it when there is no room), with two `sm` buttons: **Highlight** and **Highlight + note**. Hide it on scroll, on Escape, and when the selection collapses. On iOS the native selection menu will also appear; place yours so they do not overlap (test with your browser tool's mobile emulation).
- Mobile: highlights must be creatable and editable with touch at 420px wide; the panel/drawer (whichever option) must not cover the selection toolbar.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

1. Highlights can be created from a text selection in the article, render in the one light-yellow token, persist, re-anchor correctly after reload (including repeated text and multi-node ranges), can carry a note, and can be removed; orphaned highlights degrade gracefully.
2. The link-level note is always discoverable on the link page through a labeled control (no hidden bottom bar), auto-saves with the same debounce semantics as before, and is backward compatible at the API.
3. Links with notes or highlights are recognizable on cards, and the Notes & highlights view shows note text and highlight quotes with jump-to-highlight.
4. Notes and highlights are searchable; the text-index migration is idempotent and tested.
5. The assistant can read notes and highlights for a link.
6. All Go and JS tests pass, Swagger/README/AGENTS/DESIGN are updated, and the UI follows DESIGN.md (tokens only, flat surfaces, tint hovers, labeled controls, AA contrast on yellow).
